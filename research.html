Eleven papers

Research and sources

Every population-level claim on this site traces to one of these. Each has a plain-language summary here and a DOI to the paper itself.

The papers, newest first

  • 2026

    Islamic-period Ibiza reveals North African gene flow

    Genomes from medieval Ibiza burials (950–1150 CE) show North African ancestry arriving during the Umayyad and Almoravid periods — movement across the western Mediterranean running north, not south.

    Rodríguez-Varela et al. · Nature Communications · doi:10.1038/s41467-026-70615-9

  • 2026

    North African maternal diversity, from 869 mitogenomes

    A cohesive Maghreb maternal core, overlaid by historical female-mediated migrations. Directly relevant to how a lineage like H1 sits in the region.

    Boumajdi et al. · Mammalian Genome · doi:10.1007/s00335-026-10209-4

  • 2025

    Ancient DNA from the Green Sahara

    Genomes from Takarkori in Libya, around 7,000 years ago, reveal a deeply divergent North African lineage that had been largely isolated — from a period when the Sahara was grassland.

    Salem et al. · Nature · doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08793-7

  • 2025

    The eastern Maghreb kept its foragers

    Ancient DNA from Algeria and Tunisia shows strong continuity of local forager ancestry through the Neolithic transition — farming arrived without replacing the people.

    Lipson et al. · Nature · doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08699-4

  • 2025

    Punic people were genetically diverse, and barely Levantine

    Genome-wide data from 210 individuals finds Carthaginian-era populations dominated by local and Mediterranean ancestry, with almost no Levantine contribution — culture spread without much migration.

    Reich et al. · Nature · doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08913-3

  • 2025

    The Guanches before European contact

    Demographic history of the indigenous Canarian Amazigh, whose ancestors sailed from North Africa. The same paternal branch as this lineage has been reported among them.

    Santana et al. · Scientific Reports · doi:10.1038/s41598-025-04302-y

  • 2025

    733 modern and 43 ancient mitogenomes

    A large survey of North African maternal lineages, including the H1 subclade patterns that underlie the maternal half of this lineage.

    Colombo et al. · Scientific Reports · doi:10.1038/s41598-025-12209-x

  • 2024

    Amazigh genomic heterogeneity, village by village

    The largest Amazigh genomic dataset to date finds diversity at a microgeographical scale, two trans-Saharan admixture waves, and deep Epipaleolithic roots.

    Vilà-Valls et al. · Scientific Reports · doi:10.1038/s41598-024-60568-8

  • 2024

    Amazigh and Arab populations diverged over 20,000 years ago

    Whole-genome demographic modelling separates deep Epipaleolithic Amazigh origins from much later Arabization gene flow, via a soft-split model.

    Serradell et al. · Genome Biology · doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03341-4

  • 2023

    The Maghreb Neolithic began with migrants from Iberia

    Ancient DNA from Neolithic Morocco confirms migration across the Strait of Gibraltar — the ancient-DNA reinforcement of the scenario that best explains this maternal line.

    Villalba-Mouco et al. · Nature · doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06166-6

  • 2017

    E-M81 is far younger than anyone expected

    Whole Y-chromosome sequencing dates the defining Amazigh paternal marker to roughly 2,000–3,000 years ago, with a star-like pattern indicating rapid expansion from a small founder group.

    Solé-Morata et al. · Scientific Reports · doi:10.1038/s41598-017-16271-y

About these summaries

Each summary is written here, not taken from the paper's abstract. They are an aid to reading, not a substitute for it — where a summary and the paper disagree, the paper is right. Follow the DOI.

Papers are included because they bear on North African population history, not because they support a conclusion. Where they disagree with each other — and several do — the limitations page says so.
